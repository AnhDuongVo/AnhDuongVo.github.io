The flagship project of my PhD. I studied how the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) supports flexible, adaptive behavior in a two-tone active avoidance paradigm, where an animal must learn, and then re-learn, which cue predicts danger.

## Work

- Analysed large-scale neural and behavioral time-series from the mPFC during learning and reversal.
- Identified "transfer neurons," a population whose activity supports generalization across changing task contexts.
- Built machine learning models for movement and behavior prediction, and for representational analysis of the neural data, including self-supervised methods for limited-data regimes.
- Engineered the analysis pipeline for scale, reducing one computation from 30 days to 1 day (a 30x speed-up).
- Manuscript in preparation (Vo, Ehret et al.).

## Why it is relevant beyond neuroscience

- Transfer and generalization: the same questions that drive robust, adaptable AI systems.
- Brain-inspired machine learning: representations that stay stable while the task changes.
- Interfaces: decoding intent from neural and physiological signals for wearables and assistive devices.
